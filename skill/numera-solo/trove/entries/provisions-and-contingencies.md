# Provisions and contingencies (IAS 37)

When a liability of uncertain timing or amount goes on the balance sheet versus when it is merely disclosed in the notes. Cross-cutting domain knowledge under IAS 37 (US GAAP equivalent is ASC 450, similar in spirit but the thresholds differ); the mechanics hold on any ledger.

## The three recognition tests
A provision is recognised ONLY when all three hold at the same time: (1) there is a PRESENT obligation (legal or constructive) arising from a PAST event, (2) an outflow of resources is PROBABLE, and (3) the amount can be RELIABLY ESTIMATED. A "past event" means the obligating event has already happened, the entity has no realistic alternative but to settle, and the obligation exists independently of future actions. "Probable" under IAS 37 means more likely than not (greater than 50%). Note that US GAAP (ASC 450) uses the word "probable" to mean "likely to occur," which is generally interpreted as a meaningfully higher threshold than IAS 37's simple >50% test, so the same fact pattern can be a provision under IFRS but only a disclosure under US GAAP. If all three are met you must recognise; it is not optional. If any one fails, you fall back to a contingent liability (disclose) or, if remote, ignore entirely.

## The journal and measurement
Recognising a provision: DR Expense (or the relevant cost line, P&L) / CR Provision (a liability). Settlement later: DR Provision / CR Cash (or trade payables), with any over/under estimate hitting the P&L in the settlement period. Measure at the best estimate of the expenditure required to settle the obligation at the reporting date. For a single obligation the most likely outcome may be the best estimate; for a large population of items (e.g. warranties) use the expected value (probability-weighted). Where there is a continuous range of equally likely outcomes, the mid-point of the range is used. Where the time value of money is material, discount to present value and unwind the discount each period as a finance cost: DR Finance cost / CR Provision.

## Contingent liability: disclose, do not recognise
A contingent liability is NOT recognised on the balance sheet, it is only disclosed in the notes. It arises when either (a) it is a possible obligation whose existence depends on uncertain future events outside the entity's control, OR (b) it is a present obligation where an outflow is not probable, or the amount cannot be reliably measured. So the order is: probable + reliably estimable = provision (recognise); possible, or not measurable = contingent liability (disclose); remote = nothing at all. Reassess every period; a contingent liability can graduate into a provision once it crosses the probable/measurable line.

## Contingent asset: even more cautious
A contingent asset (e.g. a claim you have brought and may win) is NEVER recognised while merely probable, because IAS 37 prohibits recognising a contingent asset until its realisation is effectively beyond doubt. Disclose it only when an inflow is PROBABLE. Recognise it as an actual asset and gain only when the inflow is VIRTUALLY CERTAIN (at which point it is no longer contingent). The asymmetry is deliberate: probable losses are provided for, probable gains are not.

## Worked examples by provision type
- Warranty: products sold this period carry a 12-month warranty. Past event = the sale; because this is a large population of items, estimate via expected value (probability-weighted) of likely claims. DR Warranty expense / CR Warranty provision. Draw the provision down as repairs are done.
- Restructuring: a constructive obligation (and so a provision) arises only once there is BOTH a detailed formal plan AND a valid expectation raised in those affected that the entity will carry out the restructuring, created by starting to implement the plan OR announcing its main features to those affected. Include only direct costs necessarily entailed by the restructuring (e.g. redundancy, lease exit costs); EXCLUDE costs of retraining or relocating continuing staff, marketing, and any costs of ongoing/future operations.
- Onerous contract: a contract is onerous when the unavoidable costs of meeting the obligations under it exceed the economic benefits expected to be received from it. The unavoidable cost is the LOWER OF (a) the net cost of fulfilling the contract and (b) any compensation or penalty arising from failure to fulfil it (the least net cost of exiting). Provide for the present obligation at that amount (after first recognising any impairment of assets dedicated to the contract). DR Expense / CR Provision.
- Legal claim: a court case against you. Probable loss and estimable = provision; only possible = disclose as a contingent liability; frivolous/remote = nothing.

## Common mistakes
- **Provisioning for future operating losses.** Why wrong: there is no past obligating event, just expected future trading, so no present obligation exists. Right move: do not provide; future losses are recognised as they are incurred (though they may signal that assets are impaired and should be tested).
- **Recognising a mere possibility as a provision.** Why wrong: "possible" (50% or less) fails the probable test, so it is a contingent liability, not a provision. Right move: disclose in the notes, do not hit the P&L; reassess each period.
- **Big-bath over-provisioning.** Why wrong: deliberately overstating a provision in a bad year to flatter later years (releasing it back to profit) is earnings management, not a best estimate. Right move: book the genuine best estimate only; do not build "general" or smoothing reserves.
- **Recognising a contingent asset that is only probable.** Why wrong: IAS 37 bars recognising contingent assets until realisation is virtually certain; only then is it no longer contingent. Right move: disclose if probable, recognise only when virtually certain.
- **Netting an expected reimbursement against the provision on the balance sheet.** Why wrong: a reimbursement (e.g. from an insurer) is a separate asset, recognised only when it is virtually certain to be received, and the asset recognised must not exceed the amount of the provision. Right move: on the balance sheet show the provision gross as a liability and the reimbursement separately as an asset; in the income statement, IAS 37 permits presenting the expense net of the reimbursement amount.

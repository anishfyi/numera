# Control accounts and subledgers

Control accounts summarise a subledger in the general ledger. The subledger holds the per-entity / per-document detail; the control account holds the single rolling balance the trial balance reports. The cardinal rule: control account balance MUST equal the sum of its subledger at every close. Any gap is an error to be found, not rounded away. An AI must treat the control balance as derived from detail, never as a number to be edited directly.

## What a control account is
A GL account whose balance is the total of a supporting subledger of individual records. The subledger carries the breakdown; the control carries the summary the financial statements use. Examples: AR control = sum of all open customer balances; AP control = sum of all open supplier balances; Inventory control = sum of stock valuation by SKU/location; Bank/Cash control = the GL cash balance backed by the bank statement; VAT control = net of output VAT owed less input VAT reclaimable; Payroll control/liability = net pay + taxes owed to employees and authorities. The control is posted to automatically when detail transactions post; it should rarely be posted to by hand.

## AR control (debtors / trade receivables)
Asset, debit balance. Increases (DR) when a sales invoice posts; decreases (CR) when cash is received or a credit note is raised. Subledger = the open-item list per customer (invoices, credit notes, unallocated receipts). Tie check: AR control balance = aged receivables total = sum of customer statements. Common break: a receipt allocated in the subledger but posted to the wrong GL account, or a manual journal to AR control with no matching customer document, so the aged report no longer foots to the control. Never DR/CR AR control directly to "fix" a balance; create or correct the customer document instead.

## AP control (creditors / trade payables)
Liability, credit balance. Increases (CR) when a supplier bill posts; decreases (DR) when the bill is paid or a debit note/supplier credit is raised. Subledger = open items per supplier. Tie check: AP control = aged payables total = sum of supplier statements. Common break: a payment recorded in the bank but not allocated against the bill, leaving the subledger and control out of step; or accruals journalled straight to AP control instead of to an accruals account, polluting the supplier aging. Correct via the bill/payment, not via the control.

## Inventory control
Asset, debit balance. In a perpetual system it moves automatically: DR inventory / CR GRNI or AP on receipt; DR COGS / CR inventory on dispatch. Subledger = stock valuation report by item/location (qty x cost). Tie check: inventory control = valuation report total. Breaks: manual stock adjustments posted to inventory control without a matching stock movement; standard-cost vs actual-cost variances landing in inventory instead of a variance account; negative on-hand allowed by the system creating valuation the control can't reconcile. In a periodic system the control is only trued up at period end via a physical-count journal, so interim ties are not expected.

## Bank / cash control
Asset, debit balance (overdraft = credit). Subledger equivalent = the bank statement plus reconciling items. Tie check is the bank reconciliation: GL bank balance + outstanding deposits - unpresented payments + bank-side items not yet booked = statement balance. Breaks: a payment entered in the ledger but never cleared, a bank fee/interest on the statement not yet journalled, or a duplicate entry. The control balance is correct; the reconciliation explains the timing gap to the statement. Direct journals to the bank control with no corresponding receipt/payment record are a red flag.

## VAT / sales-tax control
Liability when net payable (credit), asset when net reclaimable (debit). Output VAT on sales is credited; input VAT on purchases is debited; the net is what's owed to or due from the tax authority. Subledger = the VAT/tax detail report (transaction-level by tax code/rate) that backs the return. Tie check: VAT control balance = the figure on the VAT return for the period. Breaks: a transaction posted with the wrong tax code, a manual journal touching the VAT control without a tax line, or a return filed but the payment/clearing journal not posted, so a filed period still shows a balance. After filing and paying, the period's liability should clear to bank, leaving only the current open period.

## Payroll control / liability
Liability, credit balance. On a payroll run: DR wages/salaries expense and DR employer taxes expense; CR net pay payable, CR PAYE/employee tax payable, CR pension/other deductions payable. These credits sit in payroll control/clearing until paid. Subledger = the payroll register/journal for the run. Tie check: payroll control = unpaid net pay + unremitted taxes/deductions per the register. Breaks: paying net wages from the bank but not clearing the liability, or booking the gross-to-net journal with rounding that doesn't foot. The control should return toward zero as net pay and statutory payments leave the bank.

## Control-to-subledger reconciliation (the close step)
For each control account, pull the control's GL balance and the supporting subledger/report total as of the same date, and prove control = subledger. Standard pairs: AR control vs aged receivables; AP control vs aged payables; inventory control vs stock valuation; bank control vs bank reconciliation; VAT control vs VAT return; payroll control vs payroll register. Any difference must be itemised and explained (a specific timing item or a specific error), not just noted as a variance. A control that ties to its subledger is a hard precondition for closing the period. An AI should run this tie and surface the line-level cause of any gap before proposing any correcting entry.

## What breaks the tie
Three classes. (1) Direct journal to the control: a manual JE posted straight to AR/AP/inventory/VAT/payroll control with no matching subledger document, so the summary moves but the detail doesn't. (2) Timing difference: detail posted in one period, control update or the offsetting side in another (e.g. invoice dated this period, receipt next; bank item on statement not yet booked). Timing items self-resolve and are listed as reconciling items, not corrected away. (3) Mis-post: right amount, wrong account or wrong tax code, or a document allocated in the subledger but posted to a different GL account. Diagnosis order: confirm same as-of date, then look for direct journals to the control, then list timing items, then hunt the mis-post. Fix mis-posts and erroneous direct journals; leave genuine timing items alone.

## Suspense / uncategorised account
A holding account (commonly numbered in the 1999 / 9999 range, but the number is a local convention, not a standard) for entries you can't yet classify: an unidentified bank receipt, an imported transaction with no category, a one-sided journal awaiting its other leg. It exists so the books stay in balance while the item is researched. Hard rule: the suspense/uncategorised balance MUST be zero at close. A non-zero balance means an unresolved transaction is hiding in the books and the P&L or balance sheet is misstated by that amount. Resolve every line to its real account before closing; never let suspense carry a balance into a closed period, and never use it as a dumping ground to force a reconciliation to tie.

## Clearing accounts and their lifecycle
A clearing account is a deliberate, temporary waypoint between two postings that don't happen at the same instant; it should net to zero once both legs land. Undeposited funds: DR undeposited funds / CR AR when a customer payment is received; DR bank / CR undeposited funds when the deposit clears the bank; a residual balance = receipts recorded but never banked (or a bank deposit not matched). Payroll clearing: net pay and taxes credited on the run, debited as they're paid out; residual = an unpaid obligation. Other common ones: GRNI/goods-received-not-invoiced (DR inventory / CR GRNI on receipt; DR GRNI / CR AP on invoice; residual = received-not-invoiced stock), intercompany clearing, and payment-processor/Stripe clearing (gross in, fees and payout out). Lifecycle: open with the first leg, close with the matching leg, expect ~zero at rest. Investigate any aged balance; a clearing account that never clears is a defect.

## Why an AI must never post directly to a control account without flagging
A direct journal to a control account moves the GL summary without touching the subledger that's supposed to back it, instantly breaking the tie and corrupting the aged report / valuation / VAT return that downstream filings and decisions rely on. The correct fix is almost always the underlying document (invoice, bill, payment, allocation, stock movement, tax line), which updates both control and subledger together. Most platforms either block or specially flag manual journals to system/control accounts (AR, AP, retained earnings, bank, tax) for exactly this reason. An AI should: prefer the document-level correction; if a direct control journal is genuinely unavoidable (e.g. a sanctioned opening-balance or migration entry), explicitly flag it, state which subledger it will desynchronise, and require human sign-off before posting. Never silently journal a control account to make a reconciliation tie.

# Automation pitfalls · what an AI gets wrong on any ledger

Cross-platform scar tissue: the mistakes an AI makes automating accounting REGARDLESS of which ledger (Xero, QBO, Intacct, NetSuite, Dynamics BC, Sage). Platform-specific edges live in the Writes and sharp edges section of each `platforms/<name>.md`; this file is the guardrail layer that applies everywhere. Each entry: the failure mode -> why it is wrong -> the right move. If an AI is about to do something on this list, stop and reconsider before posting.

## Posting to a control account via a journal instead of the native transaction
Mistake: booking AR/AP/bank/inventory/VAT movement as a manual general journal (e.g. DR Expense / CR Accounts Payable to record a bill). Why wrong: a journal to a control account moves the GL summary but does NOT create the subledger document, so the supplier/customer aged report, stock valuation, or VAT detail no longer foots to the control. The tie breaks the instant you post, and downstream filings read off the broken subledger. Right move: use the NATIVE transaction (bill, invoice, payment, credit note, stock movement, expense claim) for anything that touches a control account. It updates control AND subledger together. Reserve manual journals for things with no native equivalent (accruals, depreciation, reclasses, opening balances). If a direct control journal is genuinely unavoidable (migration/opening balance), flag it, name the subledger it will desync, and require sign-off.

## Balancing on the base-currency total instead of per currency
Mistake: checking that debits = credits on the converted functional total of a multi-currency journal. Why wrong: balance is enforced PER currency, not on the rolled-up base amount. A USD leg netted against a GBP leg can total zero in base currency while neither currency balances on its own; many ledgers reject it, and the ones that accept it carry a hidden FX error. Right move: prove debits = credits WITHIN each currency. Where rates differ across legs (clearing a foreign AR against a foreign receipt at a new rate), the functional residual goes to a dedicated FX gain/loss account as its own line, and THAT is what makes the entry balance. The foreign (transaction) amounts must also net to zero in their own currency; if they don't, it's a real underpayment/bank-charge, not FX.

## Posting into a closed or locked period
Mistake: dating an entry into a period that has been closed, filed, or rolled to retained earnings (or back-dating a current entry across the boundary). Why wrong: it silently changes numbers that were already reported, signed off, or used on a filed tax/VAT return, so the live ledger diverges from what was filed and reconciliation against filed figures breaks. Right move: post a dated adjusting entry in the current OPEN period that corrects the cumulative balance going forward, with a memo citing the prior-period origin. A reopen-fix-reclose is the only alternative and requires explicit approval and re-issued reports. Always confirm the period is open and the date falls in the intended reporting period before submitting.

## Double-posting on retry (no idempotency)
Mistake: re-sending a create-journal/create-bill call after a network timeout, a 5xx, or because the first attempt "looked" un-applied. Why wrong: the first call may have succeeded server-side before the response was lost; the retry posts a second, duplicate entry that double-counts and creates a NEW error. Retries are the single most common way an AI doubles a figure. Right move: send a client-generated idempotency key on every write so the platform de-dupes the retry (QBO RequestID, Xero Idempotency-Key header, Intacct controlid/uniqueid, verify the exact mechanism per platform in its writes doc). Before re-posting, query whether an entry already exists matching the same reference + amount + date + memo; if found, treat the operation as already done. A timeout is "unknown outcome", never "definitely failed", verify, don't blindly retry.

## Editing a closed-period source document instead of an adjusting entry
Mistake: opening the original invoice/bill/journal in a settled period and changing the number in place to fix it. Why wrong: an in-place edit on a posted entry destroys the original values; the audit trail then shows only the corrected state with no record of what was wrong, when, or why. Auditors replaying the trail cannot reconstruct the change, and any downstream report/return that consumed the old figure now silently disagrees. Right move: edit-in-place ONLY when the period is open AND the entry is still draft/unposted AND nothing downstream has consumed it (no report issued, no return filed, no payment matched). Otherwise REVERSE-and-repost: book the exact opposite, then post the corrected entry fresh. Most ledgers treat posted entries as immutable-by-policy and expect a reversal, not a mutation.

## Reclassing by deleting history instead of reverse-and-repost
Mistake: deleting or overwriting the original mis-coded transaction to "clean up" a wrong account allocation. Why wrong: deletion erases the evidence that the error ever happened; the trail must be append-only and tamper-evident so every state change is attributed, timestamped, and reason-coded. A ledger that "looks cleaner" after a delete has lost history, which is the opposite of auditable. Right move: move the balance with a reclass journal, DR the correct account, CR the wrong account (same amount, same date), leaving the original entry intact. If the original is a native document still editable in an open, unconsumed period, change the account ON the document instead. For wrong AMOUNTS (not just wrong account), reverse-and-repost. Never delete a posted entry to correct it.

## Trusting an LLM-suggested account code without checking the chart
Mistake: posting to an account code/name the model produced from memory (e.g. "6500 Office Expense") without confirming it exists in THIS entity's chart of accounts. Why wrong: chart of accounts is per-tenant and arbitrary, the number/name the model "knows" may not exist, may be inactive, may be a header (non-postable) account, or may mean something entirely different in this org. The post errors out at best, lands in the wrong account at worst. Right move: resolve the account against the live chart before posting, look it up by the platform's account ID, confirm it is active and postable (not a header/parent), and confirm its type matches the intent (an expense going to an expense account, not a liability). Never hardcode an account code from training data; always map to the fetched chart.

## Reconciling on amount alone (ignoring date and duplicates)
Mistake: auto-matching a book line to a bank line because the amounts are equal. Why wrong: amount-only matching pairs the wrong two transactions when several share a value, it "clears" a phantom and leaves the real item unmatched, and it cannot see a duplicate (the same payment posted twice both match the one statement line). Right move: require AMOUNT + reasonable DATE proximity (a clearing-lag window), plus direction (money in vs out) and counterparty/reference where available. A GBP 1,200 book entry on the 3rd matching a GBP 1,200 statement line on the 5th is plausible; the same amount weeks apart, or two book lines competing for one statement line, is a red flag, not a match. For batched deposits, the group must sum exactly AND sit within the window.

## Plugging an unexplained reconciliation difference
Mistake: writing a residual off to suspense, "bank adjustment", or a rounding account to force the reconciliation (or a control tie-out) to agree. Why wrong: a plug hides duplicates, fraud, missed bank-only items, and posting errors that compound next period; every figure in a reconciliation must be a named, evidenced transaction. A difference you cannot pin to a specific in-flight transaction is an ERROR until proven otherwise, not a reconciling item. Right move: classify the difference (timing item, bank-only item, or error), book what belongs on the books, carry genuine timing items forward, and for anything that won't resolve, document, flag, and escalate, never bury it. Suspense must net to zero at close.

## Reverse charge: netting to nil cash but forgetting it hits two boxes
Mistake: because a reverse-charge supply has zero net VAT cash effect when fully recoverable, recording it as if no VAT happened (or posting only one side). Why wrong: under the reverse charge the buyer self-accounts for BOTH sides, output VAT (Box 1) and input VAT (Box 4) for the same amount. Net cash is nil, but the two boxes must both be populated; if the buyer is partially exempt, Box 4 is restricted and real VAT actually becomes payable. Omitting it understates both boxes and misstates the return. Right move: post the self-charged output to Box 1 and the matching input to Box 4 (restricted if partially exempt), and put the net value in the correct value box (Box 7; Box 6 too for general cross-border services, but NOT for the UK domestic construction reverse charge). "Nil cash" is not "nothing to record." (UK box numbers; for GST jurisdictions the mechanism is the same but the form/boxes differ, verify against the local return.)

## Confusing a tax code with a GL account
Mistake: treating "the VAT account" and "the VAT/tax code on a line" as interchangeable, or hand-posting VAT to the VAT control account instead of letting the line's tax code drive it. Why wrong: the tax CODE on a transaction line classifies the treatment (standard/zero/exempt/reverse-charge/out-of-scope) and is what flows to the VAT return boxes and the tax detail report; the VAT control ACCOUNT is just the GL balance. Hand-journalling the VAT account without a tax line moves the control but produces nothing on the return, so the VAT control no longer ties to the filed figure. Right move: let the tax code on the native transaction line compute and route the VAT; do not manually journal the VAT control to "adjust" tax. Confirm the tax code exists and is correct for the supply (and remember zero-rated vs exempt vs out-of-scope are three different codes with different recovery rights, not just "0%"). VAT control balance must equal the VAT return figure.

## Rounding and FX residuals plugged to the wrong account
Mistake: absorbing a sub-penny rounding difference or an FX residual into revenue, an expense, or suspense to make the entry balance. Why wrong: rounding and FX differences are real, named P&L items with their own accounts; burying them in revenue/expense distorts those lines, and burying them in suspense leaves a balance that must be zero at close. FX gain/loss especially is NOT a fudge to force a single-currency entry to tie. Right move: post rounding residuals to a dedicated rounding account and FX residuals to the FX gain/loss account, and compute the residual as the difference between already-rounded functional amounts, so the plug equals exactly what makes the entry balance. Never round the foreign (transaction) amount; it is contractual and must net to zero in its own currency.

## Assuming accrual basis when the entity reports cash basis
Mistake: booking accruals, prepayment releases, and revenue-when-earned for an entity that actually reports on a CASH basis. Why wrong: on a cash basis, income and expense are recognised when money moves, not when the economic event occurs; posting accrual-basis adjustments (accrue unbilled expense, defer prepaid cost, recognise unbilled revenue) restates the books onto a basis the entity does not use and misstates the period and any cash-basis tax/VAT return. The reverse error, applying cash timing on an accrual entity, strands cutoff. Right move: confirm the entity's reporting basis (and the VAT scheme, e.g. cash accounting scheme vs standard) before posting any timing entry. On cash basis, skip accruals/deferrals and recognise on settlement; on accrual basis, recognise on the economic event and accrue/defer across the cutoff. The basis is an entity-level fact to check, never an assumption.

## Treating a balance as a number to edit rather than a derived total
Mistake (the meta-pitfall): seeing a control balance, a reconciliation total, or a return figure that is "off" and journalling that line directly to make it match the expected number. Why wrong: control balances, reconciled cash, and return totals are DERIVED from underlying detail (subledger documents, bank lines, tax-coded transactions). Editing the summary to a target value desynchronises it from the detail and hides the real defect. Right move: find and fix the underlying detail (the mis-posted document, the unmatched bank line, the wrong tax code) so the summary recomputes correctly. The summary is an output, never an input. If you ever find yourself journalling a control/reconciliation/return line to hit a number, that is the signal to stop and investigate the detail instead.

## What an AI may do autonomously vs must gate
Autonomous-safe: matching bank/subledger lines on amount+date+direction, resolving account codes against the live chart, drafting native transactions and documented recurring/adjusting journals, computing FX revaluation, reconciling VAT to the GL, flagging suspense items and variances. Hard gate (require human approval BEFORE posting): any direct journal to a control account, any plug to force a tie or reconciliation, any prior-period adjustment or write into/reopen of a closed period, any estimate-based accrual amount, locking/unlocking a period, and final sign-off. Every write carries an idempotency key and a memo stating what, why, the reference, and (if reversing) the reversal date. When in doubt, propose and flag, do not post.

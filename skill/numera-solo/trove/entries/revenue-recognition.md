# Revenue recognition and deferred revenue

When to recognise revenue, and how deferred revenue unwinds. Cross-cutting domain
knowledge under ASC 606 / IFRS 15; the mechanics hold on any ledger.

## Recognise when earned, not when invoiced or paid
Revenue is recognised when the performance obligation is satisfied (control of the
good or service passes to the customer), NOT when you invoice and NOT when cash
arrives. Cash received before the work is done is a liability (deferred / unearned
revenue), and work done before billing is an asset (accrued / unbilled revenue or
a contract asset). Confusing invoicing or cash with earning is the most common
revenue error and it misstates the period.

## Deferred revenue: a liability released over the service period
When a customer pays up front for a service delivered over time (an annual
subscription, a support contract, a prepaid retainer), the receipt is NOT revenue
yet: DR cash, CR deferred revenue (a liability). Each period you recognise the
earned slice: DR deferred revenue, CR revenue, releasing the liability straight to
the P&L over the contract term - the mirror image of a prepayment on the cost side.
At any point the deferred-revenue balance equals the unearned remainder; it should
foot to the schedule of open contracts, and it must reach zero exactly at the end
of the term (the final period absorbs any rounding).

## The five steps (ASC 606 / IFRS 15)
Identify the contract, identify the distinct performance obligations, determine the
transaction price, allocate the price to the obligations, and recognise revenue as
each obligation is satisfied. Recognition is either OVER TIME (the customer
consumes as you perform - most subscriptions and services, recognised by a measure
of progress) or at a POINT IN TIME (control transfers at a moment - most product
sales, on delivery). Variable consideration (discounts, refunds, usage) is
estimated and constrained so you do not recognise revenue you may have to reverse.

## Common mistakes
- **Recognising on cash or on invoice** rather than on delivery/performance.
- **Booking the full upfront payment to revenue** instead of deferring and
  releasing it over the term - overstates this period, understates later ones.
- **Forgetting to release the deferral** each period, so deferred revenue grows and
  revenue is understated.
- **One lump for a multi-element contract** - allocate the price across the distinct
  obligations and recognise each on its own pattern.
- **Netting a refund against revenue silently** - estimate and constrain variable
  consideration; a refund liability is its own line.

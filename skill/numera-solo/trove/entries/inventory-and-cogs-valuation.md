# Inventory valuation and cost of goods sold

How to value stock and move its cost into the P&L on any ledger: inventory is an asset carried at cost, and cost of goods sold (COGS) is the cost of the units sold, recognised in the same period as the revenue they generated. Cross-cutting domain knowledge; platform-specific item-tracking flags live in each system's docs.

## Inventory is an asset at cost; COGS is the cost of what sold
Unsold stock sits on the balance sheet as a current asset at its purchase or production cost, NOT at selling price. When units sell, their cost transfers out of inventory and into the P&L as COGS, matched to the revenue from the same sale. The core identity is: opening inventory + purchases - closing inventory = COGS. Gross profit = revenue - COGS. Cost includes the price paid plus costs of bringing the stock to its present location and condition (freight-in, import duty, direct labour and production overhead for manufactured goods); it excludes selling costs, storage of finished goods, and recoverable VAT. The asset only ever holds cost; profit is recognised when you sell, never when you buy or hold.

## FIFO vs weighted-average cost
When unit costs vary, you need a cost-flow assumption to decide which cost leaves on a sale. FIFO (first-in, first-out) assumes the oldest units sell first, so COGS uses the earliest costs and closing inventory holds the most recent (closest to current) costs. Weighted-average recomputes a running average cost per unit (total cost / total units on hand) and applies that to each sale. In rising prices, FIFO gives lower COGS and higher profit and inventory; weighted-average smooths the swings. Both are permitted under IFRS and US GAAP. LIFO (last-in, first-out) is allowed under US GAAP but banned under IFRS, so avoid it unless the entity is specifically US GAAP. Pick one method per inventory class and apply it consistently; the journal direction is identical regardless of method, only the amount differs.

## The COGS journal and perpetual vs periodic
The cost transfer is always: DR Cost of goods sold (P&L expense) / CR Inventory (asset). Under a PERPETUAL system the ledger posts this entry on every sale, so inventory and COGS are live at all times; this is the norm for any system tracking items. Under a PERIODIC system no COGS is posted during the period (purchases go to a purchases account), and COGS is derived only at period end from a physical stock count via opening + purchases - closing. Either way, a stock count still drives an adjustment for shrinkage, theft or breakage: DR COGS (or a stock-loss/shrinkage account) / CR Inventory for the shortfall. Never let a perpetual balance diverge from the counted quantity without booking the difference.

## Lower of cost and NRV, and write-downs
Inventory cannot be carried above its recoverable amount, so it is measured at the LOWER of cost and net realisable value (NRV). NRV = estimated selling price - costs to complete and sell. Under IFRS (IAS 2) this lower-of-cost-and-NRV test applies to all inventory. Under US GAAP the comparison basis depends on the cost method: inventory measured under FIFO or average cost uses lower of cost and net realisable value (ASC 330, after ASU 2015-11), but inventory measured under LIFO or the retail inventory method instead uses lower of cost or market, where "market" is replacement cost subject to a ceiling of NRV and a floor of NRV less a normal profit margin. When the carrying amount exceeds the relevant measure (obsolete, damaged, slow-moving or superseded stock, or a price drop), write the asset down: DR Cost of goods sold or an inventory write-down expense / CR Inventory (or a contra inventory-provision account). This is a loss recognised now, not deferred to the eventual sale. Under IFRS, a previous write-down can be reversed (capped at original cost) if NRV later recovers; under US GAAP, write-downs are generally NOT reversible. The test is applied item-by-item or by group, not across the whole pool, so strong lines cannot mask weak ones.

## Standard cost and variances
Manufacturers often carry inventory at a pre-set standard cost for speed, then reconcile to actual. The difference is a variance, split typically into price/rate (paid more or less per unit/hour than standard) and usage/efficiency (used more or fewer units/hours than standard). Variances post to P&L variance accounts: an unfavourable (adverse) variance is a DEBIT (extra cost), a favourable variance is a CREDIT. At period end, material variances are cleared, commonly to COGS, so reported COGS reflects actual cost; immaterial balances may stay in the variance account. Standard cost is a control and timing convenience, not a different valuation basis: standard cost is acceptable only if it approximates actual cost, so the books must still resolve to actual cost by period end and then be subject to the relevant lower-of measurement test.

## Common mistakes
- **Valuing inventory at selling price** - wrong, because that books unrealised profit on unsold stock and inflates the asset. Right move: carry at cost; recognise profit only on sale.
- **Forgetting the NRV write-down** - wrong, because obsolete or below-cost stock stays overstated and gross profit is fictitious. Right move: test lower of cost and NRV (or lower of cost or market for LIFO/retail-method inventory under US GAAP) each close, DR COGS/write-down, CR inventory.
- **Mixing cost methods** (FIFO on some sales, average on others, or switching without reason) - wrong, because it breaks comparability and the closing-inventory figure. Right move: one consistent method per inventory class.
- **Posting COGS twice under periodic** - wrong, because purchases already went to a purchases account; deriving COGS again from a count double-counts. Right move: under periodic, COGS comes only from opening + purchases - closing.
- **Capitalising selling or general overhead into stock** - wrong, because only costs of getting stock ready for sale qualify; selling and storage costs are period expenses. Right move: keep them in the P&L, out of inventory cost.
- **Ignoring shrinkage** - wrong, because the perpetual balance drifts above the real count. Right move: book the count difference (DR COGS/shrinkage, CR inventory) every count.
